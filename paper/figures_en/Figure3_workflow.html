C. Consequences of using the system, compared with the previous paper-based workflow
(i) Immediacy of information sharing

Previously, bed availability at another aid station was established verbally by telephone or two-way radio. With the system, every screen is updated automatically without any request being made.

(ii) Elimination of duplicate entry

Occupancy no longer has to be recorded on paper and transcribed afterwards. Length of stay is calculated at the moment of discharge.

(iii) Visualisation of triage

Each bed and chair is colour-coded as minor, moderate or severe (shown here as fill patterns), which makes the distribution of severity apparent at a glance.

(iv) Completeness of the record

Every operation is written to an append-only audit log, so the record can be reviewed afterwards without the transcription gaps and legibility problems of paper.

Clinical workflow for a single patient (A), the corresponding management screen (B, rendered in monochrome), and the consequences of operating the system compared with the previous paper-based workflow (C). Each patient passes through three stages: admission and triage, change of status or transfer, and discharge. The screen comprises tabs for switching between aid stations, a list of bed and chair states, and a visual representation of triage category; the same data can be viewed and edited from any device. The screen shown here has been reduced and re-rendered in monochrome for print; in use, triage categories are shown as red, amber and green on a white background. Patient names are fictitious and shown for illustration only.
